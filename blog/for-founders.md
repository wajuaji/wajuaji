## The part nobody warns you about

You have the idea. You have thought about it for months, maybe longer. You know the problem it solves, you know who it is for, and you are ready to build it. Then someone asks the question that stops a lot of founders cold.

"Do you have a developer?"

What follows is a process that nobody prepares you for. You post on LinkedIn. You get flooded with proposals. Half of them are from people who seem promising until you start asking technical questions you barely understand yourself. You interview someone who sounds great, agree on a rate, and two weeks in they go quiet. Or they deliver something that looks nothing like what you discussed. Or they finish the first phase and disappear before the second one starts.

This is not an edge case. It is one of the most common things that derails a founder before the product even gets off the ground.

## The real cost of hiring the wrong way

For a non-technical founder, hiring a developer is a uniquely difficult problem. You are essentially evaluating someone in a language you do not speak. You cannot look at their code and know whether it is good. You cannot always tell in an interview whether they understand your product or are just telling you what you want to hear.

And even when you do find the right person, a single developer is rarely enough. A product needs design decisions, infrastructure thinking, someone to catch problems before they reach your users. Hiring for all of that separately, coordinating between them, and keeping the whole thing moving is a job in itself. It is a job that pulls you away from the parts of your business only you can do.

When you bring in an agency, you are not hiring a person. You are plugging into a team that already works together, already has processes, and has already solved most of the problems your product is going to run into.

You do not coordinate between a designer and a developer. You do not chase anyone for updates. You do not find out three months in that a technical decision made early on is going to cost you later. That is all on us.

## When is the right time to reach out

If you are still in the idea stage, that is a perfectly good time to talk. A lot of founders wait until they feel like they have everything figured out before approaching anyone technical. The truth is that an early conversation can save you from building in the wrong direction before a single line of code is written.

If you have already started and things have gone sideways, that is also a good time to reach out. We have stepped into projects mid-way before and helped get them back on track.

The right time is whenever the technical side of your product is the thing standing between you and moving forward.
